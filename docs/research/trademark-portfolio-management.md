# Trademark Portfolio Management for Manu Games

> Filed: 2026-06-01. Tier: WARM (annual maintenance + opposition triggers).
>
> Scope: a maintenance and expansion playbook for the Manu Games trademark portfolio. Anchored to the live TIDEWARD application (Serial 99800434, filed 2026-05-02) and the realistic 10-year horizon for an indie studio that may or may not produce a second title. Calendar-first, low-cost-first, lawyer-only-when-needed.

## TL;DR

1. **One mark is filed; the rest can wait.** TIDEWARD in Class 9 covers the flagship product. MANU GAMES (word mark) and a Tideward logo mark are worth adding *after* TIDEWARD registers — not before — to avoid stacking pending applications during the same exam cycle.
2. **Maintenance is calendarable, not complicated.** Two filings keep a US registration alive forever: Section 8 between years 5-6 and combined Section 8 and 9 every ten years. Combined fees in 2026 are $325 per class for Section 8 and $650 per class for the 10-year combined filing. Put both on the calendar the day registration issues.
3. **Skip watch services until there is a brand worth watching.** USPTO watch service pricing starts at $99 per mark per year (Hawthorn) and runs $200-500 per mark per year at most boutique providers. Worth it once Tideward has measurable brand equity (target: 1 year post-launch with $X revenue). Pre-launch, the USPTO scammer pattern (see scams memory) suggests Seth is already on every harvester list anyway — formal watch adds little.
4. **International (Madrid Protocol) is a Year 2-3 decision, not a launch decision.** Base filing fee is 653 CHF (~$735) plus per-country fees. UK, EU, JP, KR, CA, AU is the typical indie six-country bundle. Skip until international revenue passes a threshold worth defending.
5. **Opposition response is straightforward but time-sensitive.** Thirty days to answer at TTAB. Hire an attorney ($1-3k retainer). Default judgment if you ignore it.

## Key Renewal Dates

US registrations require two maintenance touchpoints to stay alive indefinitely. The clock starts on the date of registration (not the filing date).

| Filing | Window opens | Window closes | Grace period | 2026 fee per class | What it proves |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Section 8 declaration | 5th anniversary | 6th anniversary | +6 months (+$100/class surcharge) | $325 | Continued use in commerce |
| Section 15 (incontestability) | 5 years continuous use, after registration | +1 year window | none | $250 | Optional. Makes the mark "conclusive evidence" of validity. |
| Combined Section 8 and 9 | 9th anniversary | 10th anniversary | +6 months (+$200/class surcharge) | $650 | Continued use plus 10-year renewal. Repeat every 10 years. |

**Filing mechanics:** Both filings go through the USPTO TEAS portal with a verified statement of continued use plus a specimen (a real-world showing of the mark, like an App Store listing screenshot). The 5-year Section 8 is often combined with the optional Section 15 incontestability filing in a single TEAS form (Combined Sections 8 and 15) — total fee $575 per class. Doing both at once is the conventional move once a mark has actually been in continuous use for five years.

**Grace period cost:** Missing the deadline does not kill the mark. There is a six-month grace window with a per-class surcharge. Missing the grace window does kill it — the registration goes abandoned and TIDEWARD is back to the open USPTO database.

## Watch Services

Trademark watch services scan USPTO (and optionally state, EUIPO, WIPO, social media, domain registrars) for new filings or uses similar to your mark and alert you. They exist on a price spectrum:

| Tier | Provider examples | Cost / mark / year | Scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| Budget | Hawthorn Law (\$99 covers up to 5 marks), Markify, The Trademark Firm (\$490 for 50-state quarterly) | $99-490 | USPTO only |
| Mid | Bonamark, Alt Legal, TM Express | $300-800 | USPTO + state + some intl |
| Premium | Corsearch, Clarivate, Questel | $1,000-3,000+ | Global incl. social + domain + image marks |

**For Manu Games today:** skip. The TIDEWARD application is fresh and the brand has no commercial footprint yet. A copycat is unlikely to bother. Revisit at T+12 months post-launch, when there is real revenue to protect. At that point, Hawthorn's $99 tier is the obvious starter — USPTO-only is fine for a US-focused indie.

**When watch matters most:** the 30-day opposition window after USPTO publication. A watch service flags conflicting applications during that window so you can file an opposition (or extension request) before the application registers. Without a watch, you are relying on accidental discovery, which usually fails.

## USPTO Impersonator Scams (per memory)

Per [`reference_uspto_trademark_scams.md`](../../../memory/reference_uspto_trademark_scams.md): the TIDEWARD application is being targeted by USPTO impersonator scam mail, first confirmed 2026-05-06 (T+4 days from filing). The TEAS database is public; scammers harvest within days.

**Recognize, do not engage:**
- Only `@uspto.gov` is real. `.live`, `.com`, `.co`, `.org` impersonators are all common.
- "Verification appointment" or "scheduled call with examining attorney" is not a real process.
- Examining-attorney assignment takes 6-9 months. Any "examiner is calling you" claim within that window is auto-fake.
- The only real status sources are TSDR (`https://tsdr.uspto.gov/#caseNumber=99800434`) and MyUSPTO.
- Forward suspected scams to `TMScams@uspto.gov` and `reportfraud.ftc.gov`. Never call numbers from the email, never click links, never wire money.

**Pay only USPTO directly,** through TEAS. Any third party demanding a fee for "registering the mark with state Secretaries of State," "federal monetary commitment," or "publication fee verification" is fake. None of those are real USPTO steps.

## Defensive Registrations

The indie game corpus skews thin on trademark protection. Some high-profile titles (Hotline Miami, Untitled Goose Game, Baba Is You) have no US registration at all, and several beloved games (Slay The Spire, Stardew Valley, Spelunky, Undertale) have been targeted by third-party squatters who registered the title themselves. The pattern: founders defer trademark work as "later" until someone else files it for them.

**The defensive priority order for Manu Games:**

1. **TIDEWARD word mark** — done (Serial 99800434).
2. **MANU GAMES word mark** — file after TIDEWARD registers (~Q1 2027). Filing fee $350-400/class via TEAS Standard. Class 9 for publisher of game software; consider Class 41 if the publisher hub starts producing content. Cost ceiling: ~$800 total in two classes.
3. **TIDEWARD logo mark** — file once the icon is genuinely final and not expected to redesign. Cost: another ~$350-400.
4. **Other-game word marks** — file each future game's title as soon as it has a public name and a credible release plan. Don't speculatively register working titles that may change.

**Anti-priority (do not bother):**
- Tagline registrations ("An Idle Almanac") — taglines rarely justify the filing cost for indies; they evolve.
- Merch class (Class 25) — defer until merch is real revenue, not aspirational.
- Toy class (Class 28) — same logic.
- Sound mark / motion mark registrations — extremely rare for indies, expensive to specimen.

## Opposition Response

If a third party opposes TIDEWARD during the 30-day post-publication window (or with a 30 or 90-day extension), the matter goes to the Trademark Trial and Appeal Board (TTAB). The TTAB sets a trial calendar and the applicant has **thirty days** to file an answer. Missing the answer triggers a Notice of Default, then another 30 days, then judgment in the opposer's favor — cancellation of the application.

**Action protocol:**
1. **Do not ignore TTAB mail.** Unlike scam mail, real TTAB notices come through the USPTO and arrive in the TEAS correspondence channel. Verify by checking TSDR.
2. **Hire a trademark attorney.** Budget $1-3k for an initial answer plus initial discovery. Solo founders should not pro se a TTAB opposition.
3. **Negotiate first.** Most oppositions settle via a coexistence agreement or amended goods description. Litigation through to final TTAB judgment runs $5,000-50,000+ depending on discovery scope.
4. **Decide whether the mark is worth defending.** If an opposer has a legitimately stronger prior registration, abandoning may be cheaper than fighting. The 2025 TTAB rule change shortened initial answer time, so move fast.

**Filing an opposition (the other side):** if Manu Games ever needs to oppose someone else's application, the fee is $600 per class. Skip unless infringement is clear and damaging.

## Cease & Desist Protocol

If you discover someone else using "TIDEWARD" or "MANU GAMES" outside the USPTO system (a website, a Steam page, an indie game in a different jurisdiction), do not draft a cease-and-desist email yourself. The indie game community treats founder-authored C&Ds as PR disasters with predictable regularity.

**Protocol:**
1. **Investigate first.** Is the use commercial? Is it in a related class? Is it likely to cause consumer confusion? An unrelated band named "Tideward" in 2018 is not infringement.
2. **Document evidence.** Screenshots with dates, Wayback Machine entries, public statements of intent to use the name commercially.
3. **Send through an attorney.** A polite, professionally drafted letter from a trademark attorney costs $500-1,500 and resolves 80%+ of real conflicts without further escalation.
4. **Public posture matters.** If the matter becomes public, the founder's tone determines the news cycle. Avoid bullying language. The Mojang vs Scrolls / Bethesda episode is the canonical warning.
5. **Do not escalate prematurely.** A small game studio publicly bullying a smaller party costs more in brand damage than the infringement itself.

## International Strategy

**Phase 1 (now through ~Year 2): US only.** US-only protection is enough while revenue is dominantly US. The Madrid Protocol designates other countries from the US "home" registration, so US must be registered first regardless.

**Phase 2 (~Year 2-3): Madrid Protocol to a six-country bundle.**

Most indie game studios that file internationally pick from this set: United Kingdom, European Union, Japan, South Korea, Canada, Australia. Disco Elysium, Stardew Valley, Spelunky, Terraria, and Yooka-Laylee have used WIPO's Madrid System; most other indie titles have not.

**Cost components (2026 WIPO fee schedule):**
- Basic fee: 653 CHF (~$735) black-and-white, 903 CHF (~$1,015) color
- Supplementary fee: 100 CHF per class over three
- Designation fees: vary by country, paid in CHF. Some countries (Japan, Korea, EU) charge individual fees that replace the standard complementary fee.

A typical 6-country, 1-class filing with the standard fee structure runs $2,500-5,000 all-in including the US base fee adjustment. Renewals come every 10 years and are cheaper than initial filings.

**Trigger for Phase 2:** international revenue >$10k/year (App Store data makes this trivially measurable in App Store Connect) or a concrete piracy/copycat sighting in a major non-US market.

**Phase 3 (Year 4+ if relevant):** China is the big "do we or don't we" decision for any game with meaningful Asian revenue. Chinese trademark law is first-to-file and squatters are aggressive. If Tideward ever has measurable Chinese revenue (or even significant Chinese-speaking Steam wishlist activity), file directly in China (CNIPA) rather than via Madrid — Madrid coverage of China has known enforcement weaknesses.

## Annual Audit

Each January, run a 30-minute portfolio review:

1. **Confirm TSDR status on every active mark.** Pending applications should show movement; registered marks should show "Live."
2. **Calendar upcoming filings.** Any Section 8, Section 15, or combined Section 8+9 falling in the next 18 months goes on the calendar with three reminders (12 months out, 6 months out, 30 days out).
3. **Check for office actions.** Any pending application's TSDR record should be scanned for office action correspondence. Office actions have 3-6 month response windows that are easy to miss.
4. **Review the year's infringement sightings.** Most indie studios accumulate 1-3 "is this infringement?" moments per year. January is a good batch-processing slot.
5. **Decide on this year's new filings.** Did a new game title clear naming? Did a logo stabilize? File or don't, with a reason on record.
6. **Verify watch service coverage** (once subscribed). Make sure marks added during the year are also on the watch.

**Audit output:** a one-page status table in this file (or a sibling file) with each mark, status, next deadline, and any action items.

## Class Coverage

US trademark filings designate one or more "international classes" of goods and services. Each class costs separately. For an indie game studio, the relevant classes:

| Class | Covers | Should TIDEWARD have it? |
|---|---|---|
| 9 | Downloadable software, video games, mobile apps, downloadable game programs | Yes (filed) |
| 41 | Entertainment services, online gaming services, providing entertainment content | Consider if tideward.app blog or related content layer grows. Not urgent. |
| 42 | Software-as-a-service, hosted software | Not relevant for a downloadable game. |
| 25 | Clothing, merch | Defer indefinitely. Only file if merch is real. |
| 28 | Toys, plush, figurines, physical games | Defer indefinitely. |
| 16 | Printed publications, posters | Skip; covered de facto by Class 41 entertainment for digital content. |

**Practical guidance:** filing in 1 class for $350 covers 90% of an indie's actual brand needs. Adding Class 41 doubles the maintenance cost forever, so only add it when there is a genuine services offering distinct from the game itself.

## Trademark vs Copyright vs Trade Secret

The three protection regimes do different things and are not interchangeable:

| Regime | What it protects | How you get it | Cost | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Trademark | Brand identity: name, logo, packaging trade dress | Federal registration via USPTO | $350+/class to file, $325/class to maintain | Indefinite with maintenance |
| Copyright | Code, art, music, written text, audiovisual content | Automatic on creation; optional federal registration for enforcement leverage | $0 automatic; $45 per work to register | Life of author + 70 years (or 95 years for corporate works) |
| Trade secret | Combat formulas, economy tuning math, internal processes | Maintain secrecy; do not disclose publicly | $0 | Forever as long as it stays secret |

**Application to Tideward:**
- **Trademark:** TIDEWARD name. MANU GAMES name. Logo marks once stable.
- **Copyright:** every source file, every UI element, every track. Automatic. Consider registering the core game (one batch) before launch for statutory damages eligibility — the $45 fee is cheap insurance.
- **Trade secret:** internal tuning math (the actual numbers in the production economy, idle math, drop tables). Public-facing UI text is not a trade secret. Internal Google Docs are.

**The trap:** founders sometimes describe gameplay mechanics as "trademarked." They are not — gameplay mechanics aren't trademark subject matter. They might be patentable (rare for indies, mostly Bandai-Namco style edge cases) or copyrightable (the expressive elements, not the rules themselves).

## Brand Use Guidelines

To preserve trademark rights, the mark needs to be used as a brand identifier, not as a generic word. Establish in this marketing repo and propagate to every public surface:

1. **TIDEWARD in branding contexts is always either italicized or marked.** Use `*TIDEWARD*` while pending (the "TM" superscript is permissible and signals claim of mark). Switch to `TIDEWARD®` after registration issues.
2. **MANU GAMES in branding contexts is always all-caps or title case (MANU GAMES or Manu Games), never `manu games`.** Lowercase reads as generic; a generic word is a forfeited trademark.
3. **Do not use TIDEWARD as a verb or generic noun in your own marketing.** "Playing Tideward" is fine; "tidewarding through your day" is not — that's how trademarks become generic ("aspirin," "escalator," "thermos" all started as marks).
4. **The trademark is the exact registered string.** "Tideward Adventures" or "Tideward: The Almanac" are not protected by the TIDEWARD registration alone unless filed separately or used consistently as the brand.
5. **Specimens matter.** Every Section 8 filing needs proof of continued use. Keep a folder of annual screenshots: App Store listing, tideward.app homepage, Steam page if it exists. Auto-archive every quarter.

## Anti-Patterns

| Anti-pattern | Cost |
|---|---|
| Forgetting Section 8 between years 5-6 | Mark goes abandoned; lose the registration permanently. |
| Not registering MANU GAMES | A squatter registers it first; you have to litigate or rename. |
| Paying USPTO impersonator emails | Several hundred to several thousand dollars per incident; the mark gains zero additional protection. |
| Litigating soft infringement pre-revenue | $5,000-50,000 to enforce a mark that isn't generating revenue worth defending. |
| Filing all marks at once with no commercial use | Application abandons during exam when you can't show use in commerce. |
| Using TIDEWARD lowercase in marketing copy | Trademark genericization risk over years; weakens enforcement posture. |
| Filing logo before logo is final | Refiling every redesign wastes $350 each cycle. |
| Sending founder-authored cease and desist | PR backfire risk; routes the dispute into Twitter outrage rather than legal resolution. |
| Skipping the annual audit | Office actions and renewal windows are easy to miss; missing them is fatal. |
| Buying a premium watch service before there's a brand worth watching | $1,000-3,000/year for alerts about a mark no one is copying. |
