# Soundtrack EP Distribution for Tideward
> Filed: 2026-06-01. Tier: COOL (post-launch + after music produced).

## TL;DR

Tideward should release a soundtrack EP, but only after a v1.0 paid launch and only with commissioned original tracks. The economics are modest (realistic steady state: $50-200/month combined) but the brand and discovery upside is real, especially on Bandcamp (the de-facto headquarters of indie game soundtracks) and Spotify's algorithmic video-game playlists. Recommended path: commission a 6-track, ~20-minute EP from a solo indie composer for $1,000-$2,500, release via Bandcamp (direct artist-to-fan, 82% take) plus DistroKid ($25/yr unlimited to Spotify/Apple/YouTube Music/Tidal), 1-3 months post-launch. Pricing: $5 minimum, pay-what-you-want on Bandcamp; free streaming everywhere else. Avoid the most common mistake: commissioning music without soundtrack-release rights baked into the work-for-hire contract. The Sound_Design_Framework already calls for commissioned music at Tier 3-4 — this doc is the distribution-side companion to that production plan.

## Why Indie Studios Release Soundtracks

**A. Revenue (modest).** A pay-what-you-want Bandcamp EP from a small indie typically clears low hundreds to low thousands of dollars total over its first year. Streaming royalties are pennies per stream; Spotify pays roughly $0.003 per stream and the 2025 payout floor change made the bottom even thinner for small indies. Standout exceptions exist (Undertale's MEGALOVANIA is reported to have generated ~£253k over 74M streams), but those are outliers, not the planning baseline.

**B. Brand engagement (audio sticky-ness).** Music is the longest-lived branded artifact a game ships. Players who put the OST on while working, gaming, or sleeping carry the brand into hours of their day a marketing campaign cannot buy. Stardew Valley's OST has 100 tracks and remains permanently in rotation a decade after launch; Hades's two OSTs each anchor Supergiant's brand more firmly than any trailer they've released.

**C. Discoverability (Spotify algorithm reaches new audiences).** Tagging a release as "Soundtrack" and pitching it for inclusion in playlists like Spotify's official "Video Game Soundtracks" or community lists like "Game Music 2026" (~99k saves) puts the music in front of listeners who do not yet know the game. The 2025-26 Spotify Prompted Playlist feature also weights "video game" intent surfaces. This is the single non-obvious lever for new player acquisition.

**D. Press story angle.** Game soundtracks pitch separately and to different outlets than the game itself: VGM Online, Bandcamp Daily's Soundtrack vertical, OverClocked ReMix, indie-music podcasts. A composer interview is its own story unit even if the game-side press cycle has cooled.

## Distribution Channels (2026)

**Bandcamp.** Best indie-friendly platform. Pay-what-you-want pricing supported natively. 82% to artist on average (15% Bandcamp digital fee + 4-7% payment processor). Bandcamp Fridays (first Friday of every month) waive Bandcamp's cut entirely (100% to artist on those days). DRM-free downloads in WAV/FLAC/MP3/AAC. Good for downloads, embedded streaming, and merch. The natural HQ for indie game soundtracks — Supergiant's entire catalog, Stardew Valley's 100-track OST, Celeste, Undertale, Minecraft, and most indie OSTs live here.

**Apple Music + Spotify + YouTube Music + Tidal + Amazon Music.** Reached via aggregator (DistroKid recommended below). ~$25/yr flat. Streaming royalties are pennies, but the discovery upside via algorithmic playlists is the actual product. Free for listeners; premium-tier listeners pay a fractional cent per stream that aggregates over years.

**SoundCloud.** Low-friction upload, free tier (3 hours of audio). Discovery is decent for casual listeners; less weight than Spotify but underrated as a no-cost top-of-funnel. Strong for full-track embeds in blog posts and social.

**Direct download via tideward.app.** Free, no royalties to anyone. Use as a backup distribution channel for newsletter rewards / referral perks. Don't make it the only path — fans expect Bandcamp.

**For Tideward:** Bandcamp + DistroKid is the combo. SoundCloud as a no-cost extra. Direct-download from tideward.app as a perk for newsletter / referral milestones.

## DistroKid (Aggregator)

$25/yr for the Musician plan, $35/yr for Musician Plus (unlimited releases plus extras like store-by-store release scheduling). Distributes to Apple Music, Spotify, Amazon Music, YouTube Music, Tidal, Deezer, Pandora, and 150+ smaller platforms worldwide. Artist keeps 100% of streaming royalties; DistroKid takes only the annual subscription. Indie-friendly, used by Jeff Van Dyck for the Unpacking OST. Bandcamp itself recommends DistroKid and offers Bandcamp customers a 40% first-year discount.

For Tideward: DistroKid Musician plan ($25/yr) is sufficient. No need for Musician Plus unless we plan multiple artist identities or schedule complex pre-saves.

Alternatives: TuneCore (per-release fees, recommended only for high-volume catalogs), CD Baby (one-time release fee + 9% royalty cut, dated business model), Ditto Music. DistroKid is the right call for our scale and frequency.

## EP vs Full Soundtrack (Three Release Tiers)

**Tier 1 — Cheap, broad (EP).** 5-7 tracks, 15-30 min. Lowest commitment for a new audience that hasn't decided whether they like the game yet. Stays under the "would I put this on once" threshold rather than the "would I commit to an hour of this" threshold. Commission budget: $1,200-$3,000. **This is the Tideward v1 recommendation.**

**Tier 2 — Medium (full soundtrack + extras).** 15+ tracks, 45+ min, plus alternate versions, behind-the-scenes commentary track, and downloadable wallpapers / sheet music as a download bundle. Big production commitment ($5k-$20k+ commissioned). Better fan goodwill, more press surface, longer-tail discovery. Standard for games with strong narrative / setting investment (Hades has 2.5 hours, Hades II has 52 tracks, Stardew has 100 tracks). **Tideward v2 ceiling — only if v1 EP earns its way there.**

**Tier 3 — Expensive, niche (vinyl + liner notes + ephemera).** Physical vinyl press, illuminated-manuscript jacket art, printed liner notes booklet, optional pin / sticker / hand-numbered insert. Commitment of $10k-$30k+ upfront with multi-month manufacturing lead time. Reserved for the long tail of fan engagement when both the OST and the game have proven demand. **Defer to T+24+ months minimum; see Vinyl Press section.**

**For Tideward v1:** EP, 6 tracks, target 18-22 min. Right-sized for a v1.0 audio package that's already deferred to v1.1 per Sound_Design_Framework. **For Tideward v2 (12+ months later):** expand to full soundtrack only if the v1 EP receives non-trivial traction (Bandcamp sales > 200, Spotify monthly listeners > 1,000, or a press feature lands).

## Tideward EP Track List Suggestion

Six tracks, ~20 min total. Names reflect the almanac brand and the gameplay surfaces Sound_Design_Framework already calls out (title, cellar, library, wilderness, prestige stinger).

1. **"The Almanac Opens"** (intro / title music). 1:30 loop. Slow ascending motif. Plays on app launch.
2. **"Sleeping Lessons"** (ambient combat / cellar bed). 4:00 loop. Low strings. Long-form to suit hours-long sessions.
3. **"Below the Embers"** (forge / smithing). 3:30 loop. Bellows-paced harpsichord and lute.
4. **"Moonless Vineyard"** (gathering / nature / wilderness map). 4:00 loop. Wood flute and pad, pastoral.
5. **"Tidemark"** (prestige / reset cutscene). 2:00 longer arrangement. Pays off the brand name and gives the press release one bold-print track to point at.
6. **"An Idle Hour"** (loop close / library / inventory). 4:30 loop. Sparse harpsichord, becomes the album's quiet-listening anchor.

Each track ships in two cuts: the in-game loop (clean A-B loop point, AAC, embedded in-app per Sound_Design_Framework) and the standalone EP master (full intro/outro, WAV/FLAC/AAC, distributed). The Sound_Design_Framework asset table is already wired for `music_title`, `music_cellar`, `music_library`, `music_wilderness`, `music_victory` — these names map 1:1.

## Pricing Strategy

**Bandcamp.** $5 download minimum, pay-what-you-want above. Individual tracks at $1 each. Around 30-40% of buyers pay above the minimum on Bandcamp; ~$6-7 effective average is typical for an indie OST EP that lands well.

**Apple Music + Spotify + YouTube Music.** Streaming free for the listener (or part of their existing subscription). Average artist revenue per stream:
- Spotify: ~$0.003 per stream
- Apple Music: ~$0.007 per stream
- YouTube Music: ~$0.002 per stream
- Tidal: ~$0.012 per stream (highest, smallest audience)

**Don't sell on Apple Music / Spotify as downloads.** That path is dying and the user expectation is streaming. The album-as-purchase channel is Bandcamp.

## Revenue Projection

**Bandcamp.** Assume 50-150 sales in the first 90 days post-launch from existing fans / newsletter audience (~2,000 newsletter subs as of mid-2026), at an effective $6 average. That's $300-$900 gross, ~$250-$740 to artist after Bandcamp + processor fees. Steady-state long-tail: 5-20 sales per month, ~$25-$100/month to artist.

**Streaming.** Assume 500-5,000 monthly streams across all platforms in year 1, weighted heavily toward Spotify. Average blended payout ~$0.004/stream. Range: $2-$20/month to artist in year 1, potentially $10-$60/month at year 2 if any single track gets playlist placement.

**Realistic combined steady state: $50-$200/month.** Not a major revenue stream. The fan-engagement and discovery value is the actual product; revenue is bonus. Plan for break-even on commission cost in 18-36 months. Plan psychologically for the brand value, not the cash.

## Production Cost

Per Sound_Design_Framework: licensed library music (Epidemic Sound, Artlist) is the v1.0 audio plan; commissioned original work is Tier 3-4, reserved for post-revenue.

**Soundtrack EP needs original music.** Most stock-music licenses (including Epidemic Sound and Artlist's standard tiers) **do not allow standalone soundtrack release**. The license covers in-product use only. Releasing a Bandcamp EP using Epidemic tracks is a license violation that can result in DMCA takedowns and platform bans. **Confirm release rights in the commission contract explicitly** — "work for hire including the right to distribute a soundtrack album on Bandcamp, Spotify, Apple Music, and other digital storefronts under the Tideward / Manu Games name, with composer credit retained as primary artist."

**Commission budget for a 6-track ~20-minute EP:** $1,000-$2,500 from a solo indie composer at $50-$125/min entry-to-mid tier per the Sound_Design_Framework Tier 3-4 row. Add $200-$500 for mastering if the composer isn't including it. Target range: **$1,200-$3,000 total**.

If commissioning is out of reach, a workable alternative: commission only 2-3 hero tracks ($400-$1,000) and fill the EP with 3-4 ambient extensions / variations of the hero tracks. Six "tracks" can be six arrangements off two themes. Pat Patrick (composer of Slay the Spire side material) and many Bandcamp-resident indie composers explicitly offer "theme + variations" packages at this size.

## Album Art

Bandcamp and all streaming services require **1500×1500 JPEG minimum** (Spotify recommends 3000×3000). One album cover for the EP — no per-track art needed. Use Tideward's manuscript aesthetic: parchment background, illuminated initial capital, oxblood accent, EB Garamond display type for the title. Keep the layout legible at 64×64 thumbnail (the listing size on Spotify and Apple Music mobile). Brief brief: "An illuminated manuscript page with the title TIDEWARD set as an illuminated initial T, and the subtitle 'An Idle Almanac (Original Soundtrack)' below in smaller scribe lettering. Parchment cream background, oxblood ink, single decorative marginalia element."

## Liner Notes (Digital + Vinyl-Ready)

Even a digital-only EP benefits from short liner notes attached as a PDF to the Bandcamp release. Format the PDF at 4.75"×4.75" (CD booklet) AND 12"×12" (vinyl jacket) so the same source can repurpose if Tier 3 vinyl ever lands. Include:
- **Per-track credits.** Composer, instrumentation notes, in-game surface (e.g. "Plays in the cellar during overnight sleep").
- **Brief Tideward lore text.** One paragraph of in-world flavor (almanac framing) per track, so the EP carries world-building beyond the game proper.
- **Composer interview snippet** (1-2 paragraphs). Press-friendly; pull-quote-ready for VGM outlets.
- **Studio credit and contact** (`Manu Games LLC · tideward.app · @tideward`).
- **Reproduction rights.** "This recording is © Manu Games LLC, 2027. Composer credit retained. Licensed for personal listening; do not redistribute." (Distinct from the game-side license.)

Designer brief: "Single PDF, two-page spread (front+back). Parchment cream background. EB Garamond throughout. Drop caps in oxblood (`#7C1F2A`). Marginalia ornaments at section breaks. Square format, 300 DPI, vector text. Deliverables: PDF + Photoshop master at both 4.75" CD-booklet and 12" vinyl-jacket dimensions."

## Vinyl Press (Defer to v2+)

**Cost.** A short-run indie vinyl press (300-500 unit minimum order, single LP, jacket + insert) is **$5,000-$15,000 all-in** in 2026, before storage and fulfillment. Lead time: 4-9 months from final master to delivered units. Plants worth knowing: Erika Records (US), Gold Rush Vinyl (US), Diggers Factory (preorder-pooling model that lowers MOQ risk), Qrates (preorder model, friendly to indies with no inventory upfront).

**Bandcamp Vinyl Pressing.** Bandcamp partners with Diggers Factory for preorder-only vinyl runs. The fan preorders fund the pressing; if the campaign hits its threshold, the units press and ship. This is the safest first vinyl path because the studio carries no inventory risk — the fans' preorders cover the run.

**Selling price.** Indie game vinyl LPs in 2026 retail $35-$50 (per the Wuchang, Phonopolis, 33 Games references in this research wave). Tideward could realistically charge $45 per LP. Gross margin at $45 sale × 300 units = $13,500; subtract ~$8,000 manufacturing + ~$1,500 shipping/fulfillment = ~$4,000 net at sell-through.

**Decision criteria for unlocking vinyl.** All three must be true: (1) v1 EP > 500 Bandcamp sales, (2) sustained newsletter ask volume for physical merch, (3) Tideward total revenue > $100k cumulative. **Earliest realistic vinyl ship: T+24 months (Feb 2029) at the soonest.** Anything sooner is studio ego, not market signal.

**Anti-pattern.** Direct minimum-order pressing (no preorder pool) before fan demand is proven leaves the studio holding 200-400 unsold LPs for years. Use the preorder model exclusively for the first run.

## Metadata Setup

- **Album title:** `Tideward (Original Soundtrack)` — Bandcamp recommended; matches the convention Apple Music's algorithm prefers for game discovery. Subtitle line on Bandcamp: "An Idle Almanac."
- **Artist:** `Tideward` — game-as-artist convention, used by Hades (artist: Darren Korb but album credited to Supergiant Games), Slay the Spire (Clark Aboud), Stardew Valley (ConcernedApe). Composer credit goes in track notes and Bandcamp description, not the artist field. Reasoning: Spotify's "Video Game Soundtracks" playlist editors pattern-match the game name as the artist. NOT "Manu Games" — the publisher name dilutes discovery.
- **Album artist (separate Spotify field):** `Tideward`
- **Composer (per-track field):** the actual composer's name.
- **Track names:** as listed above. Don't add "(from Tideward)" suffixes — Bandcamp and Spotify both clutter visually.
- **Genre:** Primary `Soundtrack`. Secondary tags: `Ambient`, `Folk`, `Game Soundtrack`. Avoid `Classical`, `New Age`, `Indie Rock` (algorithm misroute).
- **ISRC codes:** DistroKid auto-generates; do not bring your own unless required.
- **Release date:** synchronize Bandcamp and aggregator release dates within 24 hours. Submit to DistroKid 14-21 days before target release to give Spotify editorial review time.

## Release Timing

**v1 EP.** 1-3 months post-launch. Rationale: gives the game story time to develop player attachment, gives any review press time to mention the music, allows the launch news cycle to clear so the OST gets its own coverage moment. Releasing same-day as the game splits press attention; releasing more than 6 months post-launch loses the launch-news halo entirely.

**Bandcamp Friday timing.** Aim to drop on a Bandcamp Friday (first Friday of the month) for the 100%-to-artist day. Pre-announce 7-14 days ahead.

**v2 (full soundtrack, if v1 traction warrants).** 6-12 months after v1 EP. Includes the EP's six tracks plus 8-15 new tracks (so buyers of the EP aren't paying twice for the same content). Standard convention: new compilation called `Tideward: The Complete Soundtrack` or `Tideward Vol. II`.

## Tax & Accounting Considerations

Soundtrack revenue is **Schedule C business income** for the Manu Games LLC, NOT royalty income (royalty income is reported on Schedule E and treated differently for self-employment tax). Bandcamp and DistroKid both issue 1099-K / 1099-MISC at $600+ annual thresholds; both must reconcile against Manu Games LLC's books.

**Per-platform reporting.**
- **Bandcamp.** Issues a 1099-K when annual gross sales exceed $600 (post-2024 IRS threshold change). Payment processor (Stripe) handles the filing.
- **DistroKid.** Issues 1099-MISC for royalty payouts above $10/yr (low threshold). All streaming royalties from Spotify/Apple/etc. funnel through DistroKid's reporting.
- **Composer.** If commissioned for $600+ in a calendar year (and they're a US-based individual / single-member LLC), Manu Games LLC issues *them* a 1099-NEC. Get a W-9 before paying.

**Ledger integration.** Per [indenture/Research/recurring_invoice_automation_spec.md](../../../indenture/Research/recurring_invoice_automation_spec.md), the LLC ledger app tracks revenue and invoice automation. Soundtrack revenue should flow into the same ledger:
- Bandcamp payouts → "Soundtrack — Bandcamp" income category
- DistroKid payouts → "Soundtrack — Streaming Royalties" income category
- Composer commission → "Music — Commissioned Production" expense category, with W-9 captured at contract signing

**Composer rights and PRO.** Per the work-for-hire research findings: 97% of AAA composers are work-for-hire, but only 45% of indie composers (55% license instead). A good work-for-hire contract still permits the composer to collect **performance royalties** from their PRO (ASCAP, BMI, SESAC) on streaming and broadcast — those are paid to the composer directly, not through Manu Games LLC, so they don't hit our books. Include a "composer retains performance royalty share via PRO membership" clause in the contract. Mechanical and synch royalties belong to Manu Games LLC as the rights holder.

## Press Story Angle

Game soundtracks pitch to different outlets than games:
- **Bandcamp Daily** Soundtrack vertical (highest-leverage single placement; editorial, real reach to indie-music audience).
- **VGM Online** and **Original Sound Version** (specialist VGM coverage).
- **OverClocked ReMix** (remix community; long tail but cultlike).
- **Composer-side podcasts** (composer interviews via the composer's network — usually the composer pitches these, not the studio).
- **Music subreddits** (r/gamemusic, r/listentothis, r/incremental_games for the in-genre angle).
- **The Tideward newsletter itself** (a launch issue dedicated to the EP, embedded Bandcamp player, composer interview).

Don't pitch IGN/Polygon for an OST EP — they will not run it. Pitch the verticals above.

## Anti-Patterns

- **Commissioning music with no soundtrack release rights.** The single most expensive mistake — you pay full price and cannot legally distribute. Bake distribution rights into every contract before signing.
- **Using Epidemic Sound or Artlist tracks in a released soundtrack.** Same problem — license does not cover standalone distribution. Stock library music is for in-product use only.
- **Skipping ISRC / metadata.** Algorithm can't route a track without clean metadata. DistroKid handles ISRC; you handle artist, album, genre tags.
- **Releasing without album art.** Spotify and Apple Music will reject the upload. Bandcamp accepts but the listing dies on visual.
- **Pricing too high.** $10+ EP minimums on Bandcamp suppress sales; the indie OST sweet spot is $4-$7 minimum, pay-what-you-want above.
- **Releasing without DRM-free download option.** Bandcamp fans expect to own their files; streaming-only releases get worse reception in the OST community.
- **Day-of-game-launch OST drop.** Splits the press cycle. Wait 1-3 months.
- **Using "Manu Games" as the artist name.** Dilutes Spotify discovery. Artist should be `Tideward` (the game name); publisher credit lives in description, not artist field.
- **Skipping Bandcamp Friday timing.** Free 18% bump on the release-week revenue.
- **No follow-up release plan.** A single-EP-and-done release leaves all the long-tail discovery value on the table. Plan v2 / singles / remix EPs in advance even if you never ship them.

## Tideward Recommendation

1. **T-3 to T-6 months before EP release date** (so roughly 0-3 months *post*-launch given the 1-3 month delay window): commission original tracks from a solo composer found via r/gameaudio, Twine, or Bandcamp composer pages. Budget $1,200-$3,000. Contract must include digital soundtrack release rights and composer credit (`Composed by [name]`). Sound_Design_Framework's Tier 3-4 budget table is the source of truth on commission scope.
2. **1-3 months post-launch** (target a Bandcamp Friday): release the 6-track EP simultaneously on Bandcamp ($5 PWYW minimum) and via DistroKid ($25/yr) to Spotify, Apple Music, YouTube Music, Tidal, Amazon Music, and the rest of the 150+ DistroKid destinations.
3. **At release:** dedicated newsletter issue, embedded Bandcamp player, composer interview blurb, pitches to Bandcamp Daily Soundtrack vertical and 2-3 VGM outlets. Submit Spotify editorial pitches via Spotify for Artists.
4. **At month 6-12 post-EP:** evaluate v2 expansion (full soundtrack with 8-15 new tracks compiled with the EP six) only if the EP has cleared meaningful traction (>200 Bandcamp sales OR >1,000 Spotify monthly listeners OR a press feature lands). If traction is thin, hold at the EP and re-invest the budget elsewhere.
5. **Always:** retain artist credit as `Tideward` on the master metadata, not `Manu Games`. Game-name-as-artist is the discoverability convention.
